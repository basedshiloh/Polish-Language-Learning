The Polish B1 exam is the state certificate exam most people mean when they talk about "the citizenship language test" in Poland. If you are applying to be recognised as a Polish citizen, a B1 certificate (or one of a few equivalent documents) is a legal requirement, and the exam itself has a fixed structure, a fixed pass rule and a schedule set by a state commission. That makes it far more predictable than its reputation suggests.

This guide walks through who actually needs the Polish B1 exam, what is in it module by module, how registration works, where the Polish B1 exam is held, and how long it realistically takes to get there from zero. Every rule below comes from the Polish Language Act, the Act on Polish Citizenship, the 2025 exam regulation and the State Commission's own website.

> **Rules change. Confirm with the official body.** Fees, session dates and procedures are set by law and by the State Commission for the Certification of Proficiency in Polish as a Foreign Language, and an amendment to the Polish Language Act takes effect on 1 January 2027. Before you book anything, check [certyfikatpolski.pl](https://certyfikatpolski.pl/en/) and your exam centre's website. This article is practical guidance, not legal advice.

## What the Polish B1 Exam Actually Is

Officially, the Polish B1 exam is the state examination in Polish as a foreign language at level B1, called *poziom progowy* ("threshold level") in Polish. Passing it gets you a *certyfikat znajomości języka polskiego*, the official Polish language certificate. The certificate is issued by the **Państwowa Komisja do spraw Poświadczania Znajomości Języka Polskiego jako Obcego**, the State Commission for the Certification of Proficiency in Polish as a Foreign Language. Its administrative contact runs through NAWA, the Polish National Agency for Academic Exchange.

The Commission does not run the Polish B1 exam rooms itself. Exams are organised by authorised entities: universities and language schools that the Minister of Science has authorised to hold exams at particular levels. The Commission writes the papers and publishes the session calendar. The centres register candidates, set their own fees within a legal cap, and host the exam.

Under the Polish Language Act, the exam is open to foreigners and to Polish citizens who live permanently abroad. Six adult levels exist, A1 through C2, plus A1 to B2 in a separate group for children and teenagers. The Polish B1 exam for adults is the one that matters for citizenship.

## Who Needs the Polish Citizenship Language Test

The main reason people sit the Polish B1 exam is **recognition as a Polish citizen** (*uznanie za obywatela polskiego*). This is the route in Article 30 of the [Act on Polish Citizenship](https://isap.sejm.gov.pl/isap.nsf/DocDetails.xsp?id=WDU20250001611). You apply to the voivode (*wojewoda*), the central government's representative in your region, and the voivode issues the decision.

Article 30(2) is the language rule. Apart from two categories of minors, everyone applying for recognition must prove Polish at **at least B1** with one of three documents:

1. a state certificate under Article 11a of the Polish Language Act (the Polish language certificate this article is about), at level B1 or higher;
2. a school-leaving certificate from a school in Poland;
3. a school-leaving certificate from a school abroad where Polish was the language of instruction.

Since 1 July 2025, a certificate from a Polish post-secondary school (*szkoła policealna*) no longer counts on its own. A transitional rule still accepted such certificates, if obtained by 30 June 2025, in recognition cases started between 1 July 2025 and 30 June 2026. That window has now closed. If your only document is from a post-secondary school, plan on taking the Polish B1 exam.

### The residence routes, briefly

The residence conditions are where most applications live or die, so read them in the Act itself rather than in a blog post. In outline, Article 30(1) covers people with permanent residence, EU long-term resident status or the right of permanent residence who have lived in Poland continuously for a set period, usually with a stable income and a legal title to their home. The periods range from one year (for permanent residence granted on the basis of Polish origin or a Karta Polaka) to ten years. Every one of those routes still needs the B1 language proof.

### Exemptions and the other path

The only exemptions in Article 30(2) are for minors recognised through a Polish parent. Adults are not exempt because of age, length of residence or a Polish spouse. There is no "interview instead of a certificate" option for recognition.

Citizenship can also be granted by the President of Poland under Article 18. That route is discretionary. The application form asks about your knowledge of Polish, but the Act does not set a B1 threshold for it. Most applicants take the recognition route, which is why the Polish citizenship language test is effectively the Polish B1 exam.

### A note on pending changes

Proposals to tighten citizenship rules, including longer residence periods, have been debated in the Sejm in 2025 and 2026. At the time of writing, the consolidated Act still requires B1 for recognition. If you are reading this months later, check the current text before you plan around it.

## Already Have a Polish Diploma? You May Not Need to Sit It

Two groups can skip the Polish B1 exam entirely. First, Article 30(2) itself accepts a school certificate from Poland or from a Polish-language school abroad, so you can submit that to the voivode directly.

Second, the Polish Language Act lets certain graduates apply for a Polish language certificate **without an exam**. This covers, among others, people who completed degree studies taught in Polish at a Polish university, graduates of Polish upper-secondary schools with a *matura*, European School graduates with Polish as a mother-tongue subject, and graduates of some Polish-language schools and programmes abroad. The level awarded depends on what you completed. The Commission handles these applications, and the 2026 amendment changes the procedure from 2027, so check the "Certificate without exam" section of the official site.

## What Is in the Polish B1 Exam: Module by Module

The structure comes from the regulation of the Minister of Science and Higher Education of 14 February 2025 on Polish as a foreign language exams. For the adult B1 level:

| Part | What it tests | Official limits |
|---|---|---|
| Written: listening (*rozumienie ze słuchu*) | Understanding recordings: dialogues, phone calls, announcements, short radio or TV clips | Timed on the paper |
| Written: reading (*rozumienie tekstów pisanych*) | Gist and detail in everyday texts | Timed on the paper |
| Written: grammar (*poprawność gramatyczna*) | Choosing and producing correct forms | Timed on the paper |
| Written: writing (*pisanie*) | Short practical texts plus a longer piece | Timed on the paper |
| Oral (*mówienie*) | Monologue and dialogue tasks from a drawn set | Up to 15 minutes |

The whole written part may last **no longer than 190 minutes**, with a break of at least ten minutes between modules. The time for each module is printed on the Polish B1 exam paper. The oral part takes place after the written part, on the same day or the next day.

For the Polish B1 exam, the Commission's structure table gives each of the four written modules the same maximum score, with the oral part scored separately. Sample papers show a writing module that awards points for language accuracy, not just content.

### What B1 means in practice

The regulation describes a B1 candidate as someone who understands the main points of clear texts about everyday life, family, free time, school, travel and work. You can write an invitation, an announcement, greetings or a short essay on a familiar topic. You can describe events, talk about your interests, plans and dreams, and give and justify an opinion. Mistakes are allowed as long as they "do not significantly disturb communication".

The topic list is broad but ordinary: your family, your home, daily routine, shopping, food, health and the doctor, work, travel, the weather, and Polish holidays such as Wielkanoc, Boże Narodzenie, 3 Maja and 11 Listopada.

## How the Polish B1 Exam Is Scored

The pass rule is the part of the Polish B1 exam people most often get wrong. For the adult B1 level, you must score **at least 50% in each written module and at least 50% in the oral part**. It is not an average. A brilliant reading score cannot rescue a 40% in grammar.

That is stricter than A1 and A2, where you only need 50% of the written part overall plus 50% in the oral. It is more lenient than B2, C1 and C2, where the bar is 60% per module. The result is shown as a percentage for each part in the supplement to your certificate.

Within three days of receiving the exam report, the centre gives you your result in writing. You can ask to see a copy of your marked paper within 21 days of getting the result, and the law provides for a re-check of the written part.

## Registration, Dates and Where to Take It

### Session dates

The State Commission sets the Polish B1 exam calendar. Exams run in weekend sessions spread across the year, and not every level is offered in every session. In 2026, the official schedule listed adult B1 sessions in February, April, June, October and December. The 2027 schedule was published in July 2026 and again includes several B1 sessions. Always check the current list on the official site, because dates and levels change from year to year.

### How registration works

You do not register with the Commission. You register with an **exam centre**:

1. About two months before each session, the Commission publishes the list of centres holding the exam.
2. You pick a centre, follow its registration procedure and pay its fee by its deadline.
3. The centre must enter your details in the Commission's database no later than one month before the exam, so its own deadline will usually be earlier than that.
4. On the day, bring the identity document the centre names. Phones, notes and other devices are not allowed in the exam room.

### Where exams are held

Most Polish B1 exam centres are universities and language schools in Poland. The law also allows foreign universities and institutions to become authorised centres, and some sessions include a small number of centres abroad. The list for February 2026, for example, included centres in Germany and the United States. Do not assume there will be one near you in any given session. Check each session's list.

### Fees

Until the end of 2026, the Polish B1 exam fee works like this: each centre sets its own fee, but it cannot exceed a legal maximum. The Commission's page lists a cap of **€150 for the adult B1 level**, plus a certificate fee equal to €20 in złoty. From 1 January 2027, the amended Act changes the formula. The cap becomes a percentage of Poland's average wage (7% for adult B1 and B2), and the certificate fee becomes 100 zł. Your centre's website shows what you will actually pay.

## Official Sample Tests and Past Papers

The Commission publishes free practice material on certyfikatpolski.pl under "About the examination". There are example tests, sets of tasks, and full exam papers from previous sessions, including B1 papers from 2022 to 2024. For the Polish B1 exam, these are the best preparation material that exists, because they come from the people who write the real thing.

Use Polish B1 exam past papers in two ways. Early on, do single modules untimed to learn the task types. In the last six to eight weeks, do full papers under exam timing, one module after another with short breaks, to build stamina for a 190-minute sitting.

### Beware of certificate scams

Search for "Polish B1 certificate" and you will find sites offering to sell one, "registered in the database", no exam needed. **These are fraud.** A genuine Polish language certificate is issued only by the State Commission, either after you pass the exam at an authorised centre or through the documented no-exam route for graduates. Voivodes check the documents in citizenship cases, and submitting a forged document in an official procedure is a criminal offence in Poland. Paying a "fixer" can cost you your money and your application.

## How Long Does It Take to Prepare From Zero?

Honestly: preparing for the Polish B1 exam takes longer than most people hope. No official figure exists for "zero to B1 in Polish", so treat any website promising B1 in three months with suspicion.

The best public benchmark is the US Foreign Service Institute. It places Polish in its Category III, "hard languages", with roughly 44 weeks (about 1,000 class hours) for full-time students to reach professional working proficiency. That target is well above B1, so you need less. But it shows the scale. For a working adult studying about an hour a day, with real conversation practice, a year and a half to two years from zero to a confident B1 pass is a sensible plan. Learners with another Slavic language, or who already live in Poland and use Polish daily, can move faster.

## A Study Plan by Module

![An open weekly planner on a desk, a simple way to schedule Polish B1 exam preparation — Image by kaboompics from Pixabay](/blog/polish-b1-exam-study-plan.webp)

### Stage 1: Foundations (A0 to A2)

Nobody starts the Polish B1 exam journey at B1. Work through a structured course to A1, then a coursebook or a teacher for A2. Learn the cases with their most common prepositions rather than as abstract tables. Read every new word aloud. By the end of this stage you should handle shopping, directions, your family and your routine in simple sentences. Our guide to [learning Polish online](/blog/how-to-learn-polish-online) compares the main course types if you are choosing where to study.

### Stage 2: Building to B1, skill by skill

**Listening.** Daily input matters more than anything else for the Polish B1 exam listening module. Start with slow podcasts and learner videos, then move to Polish radio, news and TV clips on everyday topics. The Polish B1 exam uses phone calls, announcements and short broadcasts, so practise exactly those formats.

**Reading.** Read short real texts: notices, emails, adverts, news items, simple articles. Practise two kinds of reading: skimming for the main idea, and scanning for one specific fact.

**Grammar.** This module sinks many Polish B1 exam candidates because of the 50% per-module rule. Drill endings in context, especially the genitive plural, the locative and past tense agreement (*poszłam* versus *poszedłem*). Keep an error log and revisit it weekly.

**Writing.** Practise the B1 text types from the regulation: a private email, a formal email, an invitation, an announcement, a description, a short story about a trip, and a short opinion piece. Get them corrected by a teacher or a native speaker. Learn the formal openings and closings (*Szanowni Państwo*, *Z poważaniem*) by heart.

**Speaking.** The oral part of the Polish B1 exam is short, so every minute counts. Book regular conversation sessions with a tutor or exchange partner. Rehearse talking for two or three minutes about yourself, your plans and an opinion, and practise role-plays: a phone call, a complaint in a shop, a visit to the doctor.

### Stage 3: Exam mode (the last two months)

In the last two months before the Polish B1 exam, switch to official past papers under timed conditions. Mark them strictly against the 50% per-module rule and spend your remaining time on your weakest module. In the final weeks, do at least two full mock exams in one sitting.

## Scenario: Booking Your Exam by Phone

A short exchange you might have with an exam centre:

> **Ty:** Dzień dobry, chciałbym zapisać się na egzamin certyfikatowy na poziomie B1.
> *(Good morning, I would like to sign up for the certificate exam at level B1.)*
>
> **Sekretariat:** Dzień dobry. Na który termin?
> *(Good morning. For which session?)*
>
> **Ty:** Na najbliższy. Do kiedy trzeba zapłacić?
> *(The next one. By when do I have to pay?)*
>
> **Sekretariat:** Informacje o terminie płatności są na naszej stronie internetowej.
> *(Information about the payment deadline is on our website.)*
>
> **Ty:** Dziękuję. Czy część ustna jest tego samego dnia?
> *(Thank you. Is the oral part on the same day?)*

A woman would say *chciałabym* instead of *chciałbym*. Being able to handle a call like this is itself a fair check of Polish B1 exam readiness.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What level is required for Polish citizenship?

For recognition as a Polish citizen by the voivode, the Act on Polish Citizenship requires at least B1, proven by a state certificate, a school certificate from Poland, or a certificate from a Polish-language school abroad. Minors recognised through a Polish parent are exempt. In practice, most adults meet the requirement by passing the Polish B1 exam.

### How much does the Polish B1 exam cost?

Each centre sets its own fee. Until the end of 2026, the legal cap for adult B1 is €150, plus a certificate fee equal to €20 in złoty. From 2027 the cap is linked to the average wage and the certificate fee is 100 zł. Check your centre's site for the exact amount.

### What score do I need to pass the Polish B1 exam?

At least 50% in each of the four written modules and at least 50% in the oral part. Each part is assessed separately, so one weak module means a fail.

### Can I take the Polish B1 exam online?

No. The regulation describes an exam sat in person at an authorised centre, with the oral part held face to face before an examination board.

### Does the Polish language certificate expire?

The certificate states the level you reached in a given session. Neither the Polish Language Act nor the citizenship act sets an expiry date for it. If in doubt, ask your voivodeship office what it accepts.

### Is the Polish B1 exam the same as the Karta Polaka interview?

No. The Karta Polaka has its own language check: a conversation with a consul about language and Polish traditions. The Polish B1 exam is a formal written and oral state exam. However, Karta Polaka holders who later settle in Poland and apply for citizenship by recognition will still need B1 proof.

Start with the free A0 to A1 lessons, give yourself a realistic timeline, and use the Commission's own past papers once you reach A2. The Polish B1 exam rewards steady, boring consistency far more than last-minute cramming.
