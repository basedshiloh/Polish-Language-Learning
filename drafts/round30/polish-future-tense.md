The Polish future tense comes in two flavours, and once you see why, the whole system clicks. Imperfective verbs build their future with *będę* ("I will be") plus the verb: *będę czytać*, "I will be reading." Perfective verbs need no helper at all: their ordinary conjugated forms already point to the future, so *przeczytam* means "I will read (it to the end)."

That is really the whole idea. You already know the conjugation patterns from the present tense, and if you have worked through the past tense you also know the *-ł* forms. The Polish future tense reuses both, so there are very few new endings to memorise.

This guide covers *będę* and the other future forms of *być*, both versions of the compound future (*będę robić* and *będę robił*), the simple future of perfective verbs, how to choose aspect, other ways to talk about plans (*mieć zamiar*, *zamierzać* and the present tense), the mistakes learners make most often, and a set of practice sentences with answers.

## How the Polish Future Tense Works

English has one main future: *will* + verb. The Polish future tense splits the job between two constructions, depending on the **aspect** of the verb.

| Verb type | How the future is formed | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Imperfective (process, repetition) | *będę* + infinitive or *-ł* form | będę czytać / będę czytał(a) | I will be reading, I will read (regularly) |
| Perfective (one completed result) | conjugate the verb like a present tense | przeczytam | I will read (and finish) |

The third piece of the Polish future tense is *być* itself, which has its own forms: *będę, będziesz, będzie*. Those are the building blocks for everything else, so we start there.

If aspect is new to you, here is the one-line version: most Polish verbs come in pairs, an imperfective member for ongoing or repeated actions (*czytać*, *robić*, *pisać*) and a perfective member for single, completed actions (*przeczytać*, *zrobić*, *napisać*). Our guide to [Polish verb aspect](/blog/polish-verb-aspect) explains the pairs in detail, and this article shows what they do in the Polish future tense.

## Będę: The Polish Future Tense of Być

*Być* ("to be") is irregular in the present (*jestem, jesteś, jest*), and its future is a separate set of forms too. Here is the full table. These six words are the most important part of the Polish future tense, because the compound future is built on them.

| Person | Future of być | English |
|---|---|---|
| ja | będę | I will be |
| ty | będziesz | you will be |
| on / ona / ono | będzie | he / she / it will be |
| my | będziemy | we will be |
| wy | będziecie | you (all) will be |
| oni / one | będą | they will be |

Unlike the past tense, the future of *być* does not change for gender. A man and a woman both say *będę*. Some examples:

- **Jutro będę w domu.** – Tomorrow I will be at home.
- **Gdzie będziesz w sobotę?** – Where will you be on Saturday?
- **Będzie zimno.** – It will be cold.
- **Będziemy zmęczeni.** – We will be tired.
- **Rodzice będą w Krakowie.** – My parents will be in Kraków.

The rule you know from the present tense still applies: after *być*, a noun takes the instrumental case. *Jestem studentem* becomes *Będę studentem*, "I will be a student," and *Ona jest lekarką* becomes *Ona będzie lekarką*, "She will be a doctor." The site's reference page for *być* has the present-tense table and that instrumental rule in one place.

[The Verb "Być" (To Be) — Grammar Reference](/grammar/byc)

Note that the Polish future tense has no such thing as \**będę być*. *Będę* already means "I will be." The same goes for negatives: *Nie będę w domu*, "I won't be at home."

## The Compound Future: Będę + Imperfective Verb

To say what you **will be doing**, what you **will do regularly**, or simply that an activity will happen without focusing on the result, use an imperfective verb with the future of *być*. The Polish future tense gives you two equally correct ways to do this.

### Option 1: Będę + Infinitive

The easiest version of the compound Polish future tense: put the right form of *będę* in front of the dictionary form of the verb. The infinitive never changes.

| Person | robić (to do) | czytać (to read) | pracować (to work) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ja | będę robić | będę czytać | będę pracować |
| ty | będziesz robić | będziesz czytać | będziesz pracować |
| on / ona / ono | będzie robić | będzie czytać | będzie pracować |
| my | będziemy robić | będziemy czytać | będziemy pracować |
| wy | będziecie robić | będziecie czytać | będziecie pracować |
| oni / one | będą robić | będą czytać | będą pracować |

- **Jutro będę pracować do szóstej.** – Tomorrow I will be working until six.
- **Co będziesz robić w weekend?** – What will you be doing at the weekend?
- **W przyszłym roku będziemy mieszkać w Gdańsku.** – Next year we will be living in Gdańsk.

### Option 2: Będę + the -ł Form

The second version uses the same *-ł* forms you meet in the past tense: *robił, robiła, robili, robiły*. Here, the second word **does** change for gender and number, exactly as it does in the past.

| Person | Masculine | Feminine |
|---|---|---|
| ja | będę robił | będę robiła |
| ty | będziesz robił | będziesz robiła |
| on / ona / ono | będzie robił | będzie robiła (ono: będzie robiło) |

| Person | Masculine-personal | Non-masculine-personal |
|---|---|---|
| my | będziemy robili | będziemy robiły |
| wy | będziecie robili | będziecie robiły |
| oni / one | będą robili | będą robiły |

If those *-li* and *-ły* plurals look familiar, that is because they are identical to the third-person past forms. Our guide to the [Polish past tense](/blog/polish-past-tense) explains the gender split and the ł→l switch in full. The only difference here is that you use the bare third-person form (*robił*, *robiła*, *robili*) and let *będę* carry the person.

- **Będę czekał na dworcu.** – I will be waiting at the station. (a man speaking)
- **Będę czekała na dworcu.** – I will be waiting at the station. (a woman speaking)
- **Będą grali w piłkę.** – They will be playing football. (a group with at least one man)

![An open planner on a table beside a vase of pink flowers, the kind of page where you might write your plans using the Polish future tense — Image by kaboompics from Pixabay](/blog/polish-future-tense-planner.webp)

### Which Form Should You Use?

Both versions of the compound Polish future tense mean the same thing and both are standard. *Będę robić* and *będę robił* are interchangeable in everyday sentences, as the conjugation tables on [Wiktionary's *robić* entry](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/robi%C4%87#Polish) show by listing them side by side.

For a learner, the infinitive version is the easier default way into the Polish future tense, because it never changes for gender, so there is nothing to agree. The *-ł* version is very common in speech, and you need to recognise it the moment you hear it. Introductory course materials, such as this [University of Washington grammar handout](https://faculty.washington.edu/dziwirek/grammar/verbs/polish-past-tense.pdf), teach the two patterns side by side.

One practical tip: when the verb after *będę* is itself followed by an infinitive, many speakers prefer the *-ł* form, because it avoids two infinitives in a row. *Będę musiał wcześnie wstać* ("I will have to get up early") sounds smoother than *będę musieć wcześnie wstać*, although both occur. This is especially common with *musieć* (must), *móc* (can) and *chcieć* (want), which have no everyday perfective partner, so the compound form is their usual future: *będę mógł*, *będzie chciała*, *będziemy musieli*.

## The Simple Future: Perfective Verbs

Now for the half of the Polish future tense that surprises English speakers. Perfective verbs have **no present tense**. When you conjugate a perfective verb with the same endings an imperfective verb uses for the present, the result is automatically future.

| Imperfective present (now) | Perfective "present" = future |
|---|---|
| robię – I do / am doing | zrobię – I will do (it), I'll get it done |
| piszę – I write / am writing | napiszę – I will write (it) |
| czytam – I read / am reading | przeczytam – I will read (it through) |
| kupuję – I buy / am buying | kupię – I will buy |
| gotuję – I cook / am cooking | ugotuję – I will cook (it) |

So *zrobię* is not a special future ending at all. It is the ordinary *robię* pattern on the perfective verb *zrobić*. There is no *będę* here, and adding one is a mistake (more on that below).

### Future Tense Polish Conjugation of Perfective Verbs

Here is the full future tense Polish conjugation for three common perfective verbs, one from each conjugation group you met in the present tense:

| Person | przeczytać (Group I) | zrobić (Group II) | napisać (Group III) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ja | przeczytam | zrobię | napiszę |
| ty | przeczytasz | zrobisz | napiszesz |
| on / ona / ono | przeczyta | zrobi | napisze |
| my | przeczytamy | zrobimy | napiszemy |
| wy | przeczytacie | zrobicie | napiszecie |
| oni / one | przeczytają | zrobią | napiszą |

Because the endings in this half of the Polish future tense match the present-tense groups exactly, everything you learned about *-am/-asz*, *-ę/-isz* and *-ę/-esz* carries straight over. If you need a refresher on spotting the group from the *ja* and *ty* forms, the conjugation reference lays the three patterns side by side.

### Common Perfective Verbs With Irregular-Looking Futures

A handful of everyday perfectives have stem changes in the Polish future tense, so their forms are hard to guess from the infinitive. Some will look familiar (*pójdę* echoes *idę*, *pojadę* echoes *jadę*), but they are worth memorising as chunks.

| Infinitive | ja | ty | on / ona | oni / one |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pójść (to go, on foot) | pójdę | pójdziesz | pójdzie | pójdą |
| pojechać (to go, by vehicle) | pojadę | pojedziesz | pojedzie | pojadą |
| wziąć (to take) | wezmę | weźmiesz | weźmie | wezmą |
| zjeść (to eat up) | zjem | zjesz | zje | zjedzą |
| zacząć (to start) | zacznę | zaczniesz | zacznie | zaczną |
| obejrzeć (to watch) | obejrzę | obejrzysz | obejrzy | obejrzą |

- **Pójdę do sklepu i kupię chleb.** – I'll go to the shop and buy some bread.
- **Wezmę parasol.** – I'll take an umbrella.
- **Kiedy zaczniesz nową pracę?** – When will you start your new job?
- **Obejrzymy ten film wieczorem.** – We'll watch that film this evening.

## Choosing Aspect in the Polish Future Tense

Since every sentence in the Polish future tense forces you to choose between *będę robić* and *zrobię*, here is a practical way to decide. Ask yourself: **am I talking about the activity, or about the result?**

| Imperfective: będę + verb | Perfective: simple future |
|---|---|
| Jutro będę pisać wypracowanie. – Tomorrow I'll be writing my essay. | Jutro napiszę wypracowanie. – Tomorrow I'll write (finish) my essay. |
| Będę czytać cały wieczór. – I'll be reading all evening. | Przeczytam ten artykuł. – I'll read this article (to the end). |
| Będziemy gotować razem. – We'll be cooking together. | Ugotujemy zupę. – We'll cook (make) a soup. |
| Będę ci pisać codziennie. – I'll write to you every day. | Napiszę do ciebie jutro. – I'll write to you tomorrow (once). |

Time expressions are a good clue when you build the Polish future tense. Words like *cały dzień* (all day), *codziennie* (every day), *często* (often) and *przez godzinę* (for an hour) describe duration or repetition, so they pair naturally with the compound imperfective future. Expressions like *do piątku* (by Friday), *od razu* (straight away) and *za chwilę* (in a moment) point to one completed action, so they pair with the perfective.

In the Polish future tense, promises and offers usually use the perfective, because they are about a result: *Zadzwonię do ciebie* ("I'll call you"), *Pomogę ci* ("I'll help you"), *Zaraz to zrobię* ("I'll do it right away").

## Future Tense Polish Conjugation Cheat Sheet

To pull the Polish future tense together, here is one verb pair in every person, with the compound future on the left and the simple future on the right. If you want a single table to screenshot, this is the one.

| Person | robić (imperfective) | zrobić (perfective) |
|---|---|---|
| ja | będę robić / będę robił(a) | zrobię |
| ty | będziesz robić / będziesz robił(a) | zrobisz |
| on / ona / ono | będzie robić / będzie robił(a/o) | zrobi |
| my | będziemy robić / będziemy robili (robiły) | zrobimy |
| wy | będziecie robić / będziecie robili (robiły) | zrobicie |
| oni / one | będą robić / będą robili (robiły) | zrobią |

Two rules of the Polish future tense sit underneath this table. *Będę* only ever combines with **imperfective** verbs. Perfective verbs **never** take *będę*.

## Other Ways to Talk About the Future in Polish

The Polish future tense is not the only way to talk about what is coming. For plans and intentions, Poles often reach for one of three alternatives.

### Mieć zamiar + infinitive

*Mieć zamiar* literally means "to have the intention." You conjugate *mieć* in the present and add an infinitive, usually perfective when you mean a specific goal:

- **Mam zamiar kupić nowy telefon.** – I'm going to buy a new phone.
- **Masz zamiar zostać w Polsce?** – Are you planning to stay in Poland?
- **Mamy zamiar pojechać nad morze.** – We're going to go to the seaside.

This is the closest equivalent to English "be going to."

### Zamierzać + infinitive

*Zamierzać* ("to intend") is a regular Group I verb: *zamierzam, zamierzasz, zamierza, zamierzamy, zamierzacie, zamierzają*. It is slightly more formal than *mieć zamiar*:

- **Zamierzam zdać egzamin w czerwcu.** – I intend to pass the exam in June.
- **Co zamierzasz robić po studiach?** – What do you intend to do after university?

### The Present Tense for Planned Future

Just like English "I'm leaving tomorrow," Polish uses the plain present tense for fixed plans and timetables, as long as a time word makes the future clear:

- **Jutro jadę do Krakowa.** – Tomorrow I'm going to Kraków.
- **W sobotę gramy w tenisa.** – On Saturday we're playing tennis.
- **Za tydzień mam egzamin.** – I have an exam in a week.
- **Pociąg odjeżdża o ósmej.** – The train leaves at eight.

This is extremely common in conversation. If the plan is already fixed, the present tense often sounds more natural than Polish future tense forms like *będę jechać* or *pojadę*.

## Scenario: Making Weekend Plans

Two friends are planning their weekend. Watch how they switch between both forms of the Polish future tense and the present tense for plans.

> **Kasia:** Co będziesz robić w sobotę?
> *What will you be doing on Saturday?*
>
> **Tomek:** Rano będę pracował, ale po południu jestem wolny.
> *In the morning I'll be working, but in the afternoon I'm free.*
>
> **Kasia:** Super! Mam zamiar pójść do kina. Pójdziesz ze mną?
> *Great! I'm planning to go to the cinema. Will you come with me?*
>
> **Tomek:** Chętnie. Kupię bilety przez internet.
> *Happily. I'll buy the tickets online.*
>
> **Kasia:** A w niedzielę jedziemy do babci. Będzie padać, więc nie będziemy spacerować.
> *And on Sunday we're going to Grandma's. It's going to rain, so we won't be going for walks.*
>
> **Tomek:** To zadzwonię do ciebie w niedzielę wieczorem.
> *Then I'll call you on Sunday evening.*

Here *będziesz robić* and *będę pracował* describe activities, *pójdziesz*, *kupię* and *zadzwonię* describe single completed actions, *mam zamiar* expresses an intention, and *jedziemy* is a fixed plan in the present tense.

## Common Mistakes with the Polish Future Tense

These Polish future tense slips come up again and again with learners. Each one is easy to fix once you know the rule.

### 1. Będę + a perfective verb

\**Będę zrobić* and \**będę zrobił* are the most common errors in the Polish future tense. *Będę* combines only with imperfective verbs. Say *zrobię* ("I'll do it") or *będę robić* ("I'll be doing it"), never a mix of the two.

### 2. Reading a perfective form as present

*Napiszę list* does not mean "I am writing a letter." It means "I will write a letter." For "now," use the imperfective: *Piszę list*.

### 3. Doubling być

\**Będę być* and \**będę jestem* do not exist. *Będę* on its own means "I will be": *Będę w pracy*.

### 4. Forgetting gender with the -ł form

A woman should say *będę robiła*, not \**będę robił*. If gender agreement still feels shaky, use the infinitive version (*będę robić*), which never changes.

### 5. Mismatching the plural

\**Będziemy robił* mixes a plural helper with a singular *-ł* form. The plural needs *-li* or *-ły*: *będziemy robili* (a group with at least one man) or *będziemy robiły* (no men in the group).

### 6. Using będę for every future

Learners who discover *będę* sometimes use it for every sentence in the Polish future tense. But *Będę kupować bilety* sounds like you will spend time buying tickets, perhaps repeatedly. For a single purchase, say *Kupię bilety*.

## Practice: Polish Future Tense Exercises

Choose the best Polish future tense form for each sentence. Where a man or woman is speaking, it is noted so you can use the *-ł* form if you like. Answers are below.

1. Jutro ____ w domu cały dzień. *(być, ja)*
2. ____ ci jutro e-mail. *(napisać, ja)*
3. W przyszłym roku ____ polskiego. *(uczyć się, my)*
4. Wieczorem ____ ten film. *(obejrzeć, oni)*
5. Co ____ w wakacje? *(robić, ty)*
6. ____ do sklepu po mleko. *(pójść, ja)*
7. (a woman) ____ na ciebie przed kinem. *(czekać, ja)*
8. Za godzinę ____ obiad. *(zjeść, my)*
9. Jutro ____? *(padać)*
10. (a man) ____ wcześnie wstać. *(musieć, ja)*

### Answers

1. **Jutro będę w domu cały dzień.** – Tomorrow I'll be at home all day.
2. **Napiszę ci jutro e-mail.** – I'll write you an email tomorrow.
3. **W przyszłym roku będziemy uczyć się polskiego.** (or *będziemy się uczyć polskiego*) – Next year we'll be learning Polish.
4. **Wieczorem obejrzą ten film.** – They'll watch the film this evening.
5. **Co będziesz robić w wakacje?** (or *będziesz robił / robiła*) – What will you be doing during the holidays?
6. **Pójdę do sklepu po mleko.** – I'll go to the shop for some milk.
7. **Będę czekała na ciebie przed kinem.** (or *będę czekać*) – I'll be waiting for you outside the cinema.
8. **Za godzinę zjemy obiad.** – We'll have lunch in an hour.
9. **Jutro będzie padać?** – Will it rain tomorrow?
10. **Będę musiał wcześnie wstać.** – I'll have to get up early.

Sentence 3 shows a handy detail: with reflexive verbs, *się* can sit after the infinitive or move up next to *będziemy*. Both are correct.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do you form the future tense in Polish?

To form the Polish future tense with imperfective verbs, use the future of *być* (*będę, będziesz, będzie, będziemy, będziecie, będą*) plus either the infinitive or the *-ł* form: *będę czytać* or *będę czytał(a)*. For perfective verbs, simply conjugate the verb with present-tense endings: *przeczytam*, "I will read."

### What does "będę" mean?

*Będę* means "I will be." It is the first-person singular future of *być*. On its own it works like English "I will be" (*Będę w domu*), and in front of an imperfective verb it forms the compound future (*Będę pracować*).

### Is "będę robić" or "będę robił" more correct?

Both are correct and mean the same. The infinitive version (*będę robić*) never changes for gender, so it is easier for beginners. The *-ł* version (*będę robił, będę robiła*) agrees with the subject's gender and is very common in speech.

### Can I say "będę zrobić"?

No. *Będę* never combines with a perfective verb. Use either the perfective simple future (*zrobię*) or the imperfective compound future (*będę robić*), depending on whether you mean the result or the activity.

### Does Polish have a "going to" future?

There is no separate grammatical form, but *mieć zamiar* + infinitive (*Mam zamiar kupić samochód*, "I'm going to buy a car") is the closest match. The present tense with a time word (*Jutro jadę do Warszawy*) is also very common for fixed plans.

### Do perfective verbs have a present tense?

No. The forms that look like a present tense (*zrobię, napiszę, przeczytam*) always refer to the future. To talk about something happening right now, use the imperfective partner: *robię, piszę, czytam*.

A good way to make the Polish future tense stick is to talk about your own plans every day. Tonight, write three sentences about tomorrow: one with *będę* + an activity, one with a perfective verb for a single task, and one with *mam zamiar*. *Jutro będę pracować. Po pracy kupię chleb. Mam zamiar wcześnie pójść spać.* In a week, choosing between *będę robić* and *zrobię* will feel like second nature.
